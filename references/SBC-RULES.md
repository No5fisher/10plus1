# SBC / Dilution Rules

Use these rules whenever stock-based compensation (SBC), option grants, RSUs, share issuance or buybacks can materially affect per-share economics.

## 1. Core principle
SBC is not a free non-cash expense. It is an economic transfer from shareholders to employees. Reported free cash flow that adds SBC back can therefore overstate owner economics.

## 2. Mandatory SBC materiality test
Treat SBC as **material** if any of the following is true:
- SBC / revenue >= 5%
- SBC / reported FCF >= 15%
- Gross or diluted share count growth >= 1.5% annually before buybacks
- Buybacks are a major capital-allocation item and mostly offset employee equity issuance
- Management emphasizes non-GAAP margins that exclude SBC
- The company is a high-growth software / internet / platform business where equity compensation is structurally important

If the company is close to a threshold, use judgment and default toward full SBC analysis.

## 3. Required outputs when SBC is material
The report MUST show both:
- **Reported FCF**: company-reported or conventionally calculated free cash flow
- **Economic FCF**: a conservative owner-earnings proxy that recognizes SBC as an economic cost

Default conservative formula:

`Economic FCF = Reported FCF - SBC`

This is not claimed to be the only theoretically correct treatment. It is a deliberately conservative cross-check. If a better company-specific method is used, explain it and still show the simple FCF-minus-SBC comparison.

Also calculate when data permits:
- SBC / revenue
- SBC / reported FCF
- Gross share issuance / dilution
- Net change in diluted share count
- Buybacks / SBC
- Buybacks / FCF
- FCF per share growth vs reported FCF growth

## 4. Buyback rule
Never call buybacks shareholder-friendly merely because repurchase dollars are large.

Ask:
1. Did diluted share count actually fall?
2. How much of repurchase spending merely offset SBC dilution?
3. Was stock repurchased below a reasonable intrinsic-value range?
4. Was buyback funded by FCF, excess cash or new debt?
5. Did leverage or liquidity deteriorate materially?

Classify buybacks as one of:
- **Value-accretive**: net share count falls, price is reasonable/cheap, balance sheet remains sound
- **Dilution-offsetting**: most repurchases merely neutralize SBC issuance
- **Value-destructive / risky**: expensive repurchases, debt-funded repurchases or weak balance-sheet protection

## 5. Valuation rule
When SBC is material, valuation MUST include at least two lenses:
- Valuation on reported FCF
- Valuation on Economic FCF

For DCF or owner-earnings valuation, prefer Economic FCF as the conservative base case unless there is a well-supported alternative treatment.

If the difference between reported-FCF valuation and Economic-FCF valuation is large, explicitly state that **SBC accounting treatment is a key valuation variable**.

## 6. Non-GAAP rule
When management excludes SBC from non-GAAP operating income, EPS or margin:
- Show GAAP and non-GAAP side by side where material
- Do not use non-GAAP margin alone to judge structural profitability
- Explain what percentage of revenue SBC represents
- Check whether SBC intensity is falling, stable or rising

## 7. Per-share economics rule
Company growth is not enough. Always inspect per-share value creation.

Minimum checks:
- Revenue growth vs revenue/share growth
- FCF growth vs FCF/share growth
- EPS growth vs diluted EPS/share count effects
- 3-5 year diluted-share CAGR when available

If the company grows rapidly but per-share economics lag materially, flag this as a thesis-quality issue.

## 8. Monitoring thresholds
For material-SBC companies, include SBC in quarterly monitoring. Example warning conditions:
- SBC / revenue rises for 3+ consecutive quarters
- SBC / revenue >10% without clearly superior per-share growth
- Buybacks fail to reduce diluted share count
- Net share count starts rising despite large repurchases
- Debt-funded buybacks materially increase net leverage

Use company-specific thresholds where better evidence exists.

## 9. Reporting language
Use precise wording:
- FACT: “TTM SBC was $X, equal to Y% of revenue.”
- ESTIMATE: “Economic FCF is estimated at reported FCF less SBC.”
- JUDGMENT: “At current valuation, the stock appears inexpensive on reported FCF but only fairly valued on SBC-adjusted owner economics.”

Do not say “SBC is non-cash so it does not matter.”
